How to Read a Signal

Every field is designed to provide context, not certainty. Understanding each component helps you evaluate the observation critically.

Scenario

The market context the signal is based on — such as "bullish continuation above support." This is the reasoning behind the observation, not a guaranteed outcome.

Entry Area

A price zone identified for potential entry — always a range, not a single price. Based on support levels, breakout points or pullback zones.

Target

A potential objective level based on the identified scenario. Not a guarantee that price will reach that level.

Invalidation

The level at which the scenario is considered no longer valid. Helps you define when to stop tracking a signal rather than holding onto a failed scenario.

Risk Category

Conservative — tighter risk, closer invalidation. Balanced — moderate risk-reward. Active — wider risk, higher volatility.

Confidence Context

Indication of analysis confidence — not a probability of success. Low = conflicting factors. Moderate = clearer evidence. High = strong alignment — but no signal is guaranteed.

Signal Limitations

Signals cannot predict the future. Market conditions can change rapidly and unexpectedly.
Signals cannot guarantee outcomes. Every signal can and will be wrong at times. Confidence categories are not probability statements.
Signals cannot replace your research. They are starting points for your own analysis, not substitutes for independent work.

Frequently Asked Questions

AI trading signals are structured market observations generated by analysing price data, patterns and indicators. Each signal includes a scenario, entry area, target, invalidation level, risk category and confidence context. They are research inputs, not trading recommendations.

Signal timing depends on data availability and market conditions. Each signal includes a generated timestamp and data source label so you know exactly when it was created and what data it was based on. All signals on this page are illustrative examples.

Active — the scenario is still valid and being tracked. Triggered — price has entered the entry area. Expired — the timeframe elapsed without triggering. Invalidated — price broke the invalidation level. Closed — the signal was manually closed or the target was reached.

Signals should be one input among many in your research process. Review the scenario, check the invalidation level, consider the risk category, and verify the observations against your own analysis before making any trading decision.
